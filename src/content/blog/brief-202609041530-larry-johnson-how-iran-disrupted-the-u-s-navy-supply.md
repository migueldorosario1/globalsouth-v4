<p>In a recent interview on the channel Dialogue Works with host Nima R. Alkhorshid, the former CIA analyst Larry Johnson analyzed the mounting logistical pressures facing the United States Navy in the Middle East. Johnson highlighted a recent media report detailing how Iranian operations have severely disrupted the traditional regional supply networks for American warships. According to the interviewee, around 20 U.S. vessels carrying roughly 20,000 sailors and marines require immense weekly supplies of food and fuel, which have become increasingly difficult to deliver due to the degradation of key logistics bases in the region.</p><p>According to Larry Johnson, the current crisis is not a sudden development but the direct result of short-sighted military decisions made over a decade ago. The analyst pointed out that the U.S. Navy previously operated four fast combat support ships but reduced that number to two due to budget cuts. Consequently, with regional ports like those in Bahrain compromised or deemed too dangerous, U.S. supply ships are now forced to travel thousands of miles to bases in Diego Garcia or Singapore before returning to replenish the fleet. This immense distance severely limits the operational sustainability of the U.S. military presence in the Strait of Hormuz, making it highly difficult for the U.S. to sustain operations.</p><p>During the conversation on Dialogue Works, the interviewee also expressed strong skepticism regarding the political rhetoric coming from Washington, particularly criticizing statements made by J.D. Vance. When asked by the host Nima R. Alkhorshid about Vance's assertions that the United States never targets civilians in combat and always learns from its mistakes, Larry Johnson characterized these remarks as completely detached from historical reality. The analyst cited historical military campaigns, from World War II to conflicts in the Middle East and East Africa, to argue that civilian casualties have consistently been a tragic consequence of U.S. military interventions.</p><p>Turning to the broader regional dynamics, Larry Johnson observed that Turkey is taking a more active initiative in trying to pull together a cohesive Muslim coalition, which could potentially act as a significant bulwark against Israeli actions in the region. He noted that such a development would derail the plans of Zionist factions in Washington and Tel Aviv, further complicating the strategic landscape for the United States, which currently lacks a coherent long-term policy for countries like Syria and Yemen. According to the analyst, the lack of strategic thinking in Washington leaves the U.S. with very few viable options.</p><p>Finally, Larry Johnson discussed the growing economic and political influence of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO). He emphasized that the oil-producing nations within the SCO represent a massive share of global energy resources, controlling roughly 80 percent of the oil volume produced by OPEC. According to the analyst, this economic bloc is increasingly capable of establishing self-sufficient financial mechanisms and trading in local currencies, effectively bypassing Western sanctions and demonstrating a profound shift in global power away from Washington.</p>
