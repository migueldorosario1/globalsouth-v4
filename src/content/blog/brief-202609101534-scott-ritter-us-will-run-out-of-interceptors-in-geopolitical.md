<p>The military balance of power in the Middle East is undergoing a profound and irreversible shift as Iran escalates its defensive and offensive missile operations, according to military analyst Scott Ritter. In an interview with the channel Dialogue Works, conducted by Nima R. Alkhorshid, the interviewee Scott Ritter explained that the recent waves of Iranian missile attacks targeting US-affiliated tankers in the Persian Gulf and military installations in Jordan highlight the severe limitations of Western air defense systems. According to the analyst, the heavy reliance on interceptor missiles by US forces and their regional allies is creating an unsustainable military math problem that the Pentagon cannot easily solve.</p><p>Ritter emphasized that while the United States has rushed deliveries of air defense interceptors to Jordan to counter incoming Iranian projectiles, this strategy has a very short shelf life. In his view, Iran possesses a virtually inexhaustible supply of advanced missiles, whereas the United States and its regional partners face critical shortages of interceptor munitions. Once these defense stockpiles are exhausted, Ritter argued, US forces will be left entirely exposed, which will eventually compel them to withdraw their remaining military assets from Jordan, marking a further retreat from the region.</p><p>This tactical strain is happening alongside a broader, strategic retreat of the American military footprint in the Middle East. Ritter noted that US Navy commanders have already abandoned significant operations out of critical bases in Bahrain, Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates, and Qatar. According to the analyst, the current escalation is accelerating a planned US retrograde back to the Western Hemisphere, a shift outlined in the November 2025 National Security Strategy document. As the US attempts to pivot its resources to the Pacific to contain China, its long-term military dominance in the Middle East is effectively crumbling under the pressure of regional resistance forces.</p><p>Regarding the political calculations in Washington, Ritter suggested that the administration of Donald Trump is actively searching for an elegant, face-saving mechanism to disengage from the conflict without appearing defeated. He pointed to recent high-level diplomatic maneuvers, including reports of a nuclear-related understanding involving Russia building nuclear power plants in Iran, as potential avenues for Donald Trump to declare a symbolic victory. However, Ritter warned that the highly aggressive posture of figures like Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth is divorced from the reality on the ground, pointing to a massive, unprecedented internal dissent from senior Pentagon commanders who warn that the US is dangerously depleting its ammunition reserves.</p><p>The situation is further complicated by Israel's strategic miscalculations under the leadership of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Ritter asserted that Israel has lost its strategic gamble against Iran and is now facing severe economic stagnation and ammunition depletion. With the internal political and military crisis deepening in Tel Aviv, Israel has tried to pivot its focus toward Turkey as its primary threat, but lacks the resources to sustain a conflict of that scale. Meanwhile, public support for Israel within the United States is rapidly evaporating due to the humanitarian catastrophe in Gaza, a shift that Ritter attributes to the visibility of civilian deaths rather than foreign influence campaigns.</p><p>Ultimately, the escalating conflict in the Middle East reveals a declining superpower struggling to manage its imperial commitments. Ritter concluded that whether in the Persian Gulf, where a highly destructive tanker war threatens the global economy and local ecology, or in Ukraine, where European allies are facing an imminent collapse, the United States no longer possesses the logistical or military capacity to enforce its will globally. The only viable path forward for Washington, according to the analyst, is to accept its diminished role and allow diplomatic channels to untangle the complex web of global conflicts before they spin entirely out of control.</p>
