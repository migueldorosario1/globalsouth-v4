<p>Former CIA analyst and State Department official Larry Johnson declared the United States’ military posture in West Asia functionally bankrupt, citing catastrophic depletion of precision munitions, aircraft losses exceeding 100 fixed-wing platforms, and the near-total attrition of the MQ-9 Reaper fleet. He stated that Washington has abandoned seventeen forward operating bases across the Middle East in under four months — not as a strategic recalibration but as an emergency evacuation forced by operational incapacity.</p>
<p>Johnson asserted that U.S. air defense interceptors are now rationed to the point where Gulf Cooperation Council states receive no guaranteed coverage, and Israel’s Iron Dome system operates without American replenishment or real-time radar integration. He described the current Pentagon command structure as a “zombie military”: hollowed out by Pete Hegseth’s purges, stripped of institutional memory, and awaiting a sacrificial scapegoat to absorb blame for systemic failure.</p>
<p>The interview was broadcast by Daniel Davis / Deep Dive, hosted by Daniel Davis, on September 11, 2026 — the 25th anniversary of the 9/11 attacks. Larry Johnson served as Deputy Director of the State Department’s Counterterrorism Office and later as a senior CIA analyst specializing in asymmetric warfare and regional intelligence architecture; his assessments carry weight precisely because they originate from inside the machinery he now dissects.</p>
<p>He dismissed Trump and Hegseth’s public boasts about “controlling the Strait” as dangerous theater disconnected from material reality on the ground. Ansar Allah forces, he emphasized, are not “ragtag Houthi rebels” but a disciplined, Iran-backed conventional force with integrated air defense, anti-ship cruise missiles, and battlefield command systems tested across years of sustained combat.</p>
<p>Johnson noted that U.S. naval task forces now avoid the Bab el-Mandeb entirely unless escorted by British or French vessels — a tacit admission of lost maritime dominance. He cited classified logistics reports showing that Navy destroyers deployed to the Red Sea have fired over 80% of their Standard Missile-3 inventory in less than six weeks, with zero replenishment scheduled before Q1 2027.</p>
<p>The collapse extends beyond hardware: junior officers report being ordered to falsify readiness reports to mask maintenance backlogs and fuel shortages. Senior commanders, he said, are instructed to avoid using the term “defeat” in internal briefings — substituting “tactical recalibration” even when entire squadrons are grounded for lack of spare parts.</p>
<p>He stressed that John Mearsheimer’s “balance of resolve” thesis is no longer theoretical — it is empirically visible in every engagement from Al-Dhaleh to Sirte. Local forces fight for sovereignty, territory, and survival; U.S. personnel rotate through deployments with no stake in outcome beyond career preservation or contractual bonuses.</p>
<p>Johnson concluded that the war against Iran and Yemen has not been contained — it has metastasized into a structural unraveling of America’s global military credibility. Every base closure, every unacknowledged aircraft loss, every intercepted missile left unchallenged signals to Global South capitals that U.S. power projection is no longer credible, only performative.</p>
