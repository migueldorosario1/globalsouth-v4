<p>In an interview on the channel Daniel Davis / Deep Dive, conducted by Cyrus, the retired colonel and military analyst Daniel Davis warned that the United States is dangerously unprepared for a direct military confrontation with Iran. Reflecting on his own extensive combat experience, Davis drew a sharp contrast between the clear, achievable objectives of the Gulf War and the prolonged, demoralizing campaigns of the war in Afghanistan. He emphasized that pushing American troops into another highly volatile, unwinnable situation without a viable military strategy is not only a failure of leadership but a severe disservice to the service members who bear the physical and psychological consequences of these decisions.</p><p>According to Daniel Davis, a conflict with Iran introduces a level of danger that active-duty U.S. personnel have not experienced since the Vietnam War. Unlike previous insurgent groups such as the Taliban, Iran possesses advanced, conventional military capabilities that allow it to strike back directly against American targets. Davis highlighted the rising toll of wounded and killed service members in the region, noting that the intense trauma has already contributed to tragic suicides among returning troops. He criticized political and military leaders for downplaying these human costs, arguing that public gestures of support for the military often mask a callous disregard for the grim realities on the ground.</p><p>Daniel Davis explained that Iran does not need to defeat the United States in a traditional military sense to emerge victorious. Instead, Tehran's strategy relies on keeping the Strait of Hormuz highly dangerous, driving up global oil prices, and forcing Washington into an incredibly expensive war of attrition. Davis observed that while international sanctions inflict damage on the Iranian population, the nation is resilient and possesses sufficient domestic petroleum, water, and food resources to endure indefinitely. On the other hand, the United States remains economically vulnerable to prolonged maritime blockades, which threaten to disrupt critical supplies of diesel, jet fuel, and agricultural fertilizers, potentially locking in severe food shortages for years to come.</p><p>The discussion also addressed the domestic political landscape in the United States, with Daniel Davis offering a critical perspective on Donald Trump and the broader republican strategy ahead of upcoming electoral contests. Davis argued that Donald Trump appears to be experiencing noticeable cognitive decline compared to his first term, isolating himself from dissenting policy advice and surrounding himself with advisors who reinforce unrealistic plans. This insularity, Davis suggested, detaches leadership from a genuine understanding of complex regional dynamics, such as the Persian or Shia religious mentalities, leading to simplistic assumptions that U.S. forces can resolve complex geopolitical challenges through sheer force alone.</p><p>Finally, Davis provided a stark assessment of the war in Ukraine, asserting that a military victory for Kyiv is mathematically and strategically impossible. According to Daniel Davis, Russia's superior industrial capacity, massive reserves of battle-tested personnel, and unstoppable ballistic missile systems ensure that Ukraine will eventually be forced to capitulate on Moscow's terms. Davis warned that while Russia still prefers a negotiated diplomatic settlement that addresses its core security concerns, prolonged delays will only strengthen internal pressure on Vladimir Putin to abandon diplomacy in favor of total military conquest, which could escalate the conflict into a direct and catastrophic confrontation with NATO.</p>
