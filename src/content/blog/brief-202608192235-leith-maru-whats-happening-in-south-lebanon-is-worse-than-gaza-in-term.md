<p>Beirut-based analyst Leith Maru delivered a forensic indictment of Israel’s military campaign in southern Lebanon, asserting it constitutes genocidal erasure on a scale surpassing Gaza in both territorial scope and historical violence. He cited the systematic demolition of entire villages, centuries-old graveyards, active mosques and churches, and irreplaceable archaeological sites across a region multiple times Gaza’s size.</p>
<p>Maru stressed that this is not collateral damage but deliberate cultural annihilation — a coordinated settler-colonial project extending across Lebanon, Syria, and Palestine. He linked Israeli operations in Majd Al Silim and Al Mansouri to broader patterns of forced displacement, land seizure, and infrastructural obliteration designed to erase demographic continuity.</p>
<p>The interview was broadcast by Dialogue Works, hosted by Samira Khalil, on August 19, 2026. Leith Maru is a Beirut-based geopolitical analyst with deep familial and operational ties across Lebanon and Syria, widely cited by regional civil society networks for real-time field verification and military terrain analysis.</p>
<p>He identified Ali Tahir Hill as the linchpin of southern Lebanon’s defensive geography — a strategic high ground whose capture enabled Israeli forces to dominate movement corridors and conduct sustained artillery barrages on civilian infrastructure. Maru documented how Israeli units systematically targeted water reservoirs, olive groves, and seed banks to cripple agricultural self-sufficiency for generations.</p>
<p>Maru confirmed Israeli strikes on Turkish military positions in Idlib province, describing them as deliberate escalations meant to fracture Ankara’s regional posture and isolate Syrian resistance actors. He noted that these strikes occurred without prior warning or diplomatic coordination, violating established deconfliction channels Turkey had maintained with both Russia and the United States.</p>
<p>He argued that Iran’s red-line warnings regarding Al-Julani were not rhetorical posturing but urgent responses to Israeli reconnaissance flights over Aleppo and drone incursions near Damascus International Airport. Maru emphasized that Tehran’s restraint reflects coercion, not consent — a calculated withdrawal under threat of direct confrontation rather than strategic alignment.</p>
<p>Maru rejected the framing of Hezbollah as the sole driver of escalation, insisting that Israeli operations predated and exceeded any Hezbollah mobilization in the south. He presented satellite imagery and eyewitness testimony showing Israeli bulldozers leveling homes in Qana weeks before any reported Hezbollah activity in the area.</p>
<p>He concluded that the international humanitarian architecture has been functionally dismantled in southern Lebanon, with UNRWA offices shuttered, ICRC access denied, and Lebanese Red Cross volunteers detained at checkpoints. Maru stated that what remains is not occupation but erasure — a war waged not for territory alone, but for the irreversible deletion of memory, lineage, and rootedness.</p>
