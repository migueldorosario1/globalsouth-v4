<p>The global shift toward a multipolar world order is causing deep anxieties in European capitals, leading to what critics describe as an increasingly aggressive war mentality. This shift is not just affecting active military powers but is also heavily impacting traditionally neutral states. Switzerland, historically recognized for its strict non-alignment, is currently experiencing an intense internal struggle as political elites face mounting pressure to integrate into Western and transatlantic security frameworks.</p><p>In an interview on the channel Neutrality Studies Français with the presenter Pascal Lottaz, the Swiss Communist Party member Alberto Togni discussed these pressing challenges to Swiss sovereignty. According to Alberto Togni, Switzerland is witnessing a coordinated effort by liberal political factions and mainstream media to gradually dismantle the country's constitutional neutrality. He argued that the current narrative pushed by the state and major editorial outlets seeks to align Swiss foreign policy with NATO and European Union defense architectures, undermining decades of diplomatic mediation.</p><p>During the discussion, Alberto Togni explained that the erosion of neutrality is occurring piece by piece. He noted that mainstream political parties have launched systematic campaigns to discredit neutrality advocates, often labeling initiative proponents as being aligned with foreign adversaries. Togni criticized these methods, suggesting that the relentless stream of media propaganda aims to prepare the public background for a permanent security alignment, although he remains hopeful that the Swiss population retains a healthy skepticism toward such political maneuvers.</p><p>From a Marxist perspective, Alberto Togni elaborated on the economic and social dimensions of militarism. He pointed out that neutrality is a vital tool to reject the logic of confronting blocs and to resist a new Cold War mentality. According to Togni, a truly neutral Swiss foreign policy is an instrument of international solidarity that allows the country to diversify its trade and avoid being dragged into imperialist conflicts driven by a declining Western hegemony. He emphasized that the working class always bears the heaviest burden of war, making the preservation of peace a fundamental left-wing objective.</p><p>The conversation also touched upon the controversial role of unilateral economic sanctions. Highlighting international law, the host Pascal Lottaz pointed out that unilateral coercive measures often contradict the United Nations Charter and basic human rights. Alberto Togni agreed, observing that Swiss participation in international sanctions compromises its role as a credible mediator. He cautioned that by aligning Swiss economic policy with transatlantic strategies, the nation's political elite is sacrificing a critical tool for global peace in favor of dogmatic geopolitical alliances.</p><p>In conclusion, Alberto Togni expressed a cautious and somewhat pessimistic view of the immediate future, warning of the intense pressure to integrate Swiss defense systems with NATO. However, he urged peace advocates and social movements to continue organizing and raising awareness about the strategic value of non-alignment. For Togni, defending strict neutrality is not an act of isolationism, but rather an active commitment to global stability and a necessary stance against the destructive global arms race currently sweeping through Europe.</p>
