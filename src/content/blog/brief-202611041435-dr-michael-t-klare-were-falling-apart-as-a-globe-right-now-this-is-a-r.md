<p>Geopolitical analyst Dr. Michael T. Klare identifies the present global turbulence not as episodic crisis but as a civilizational rupture — the irreversible end of two centuries of Western-dominated order. This is not decline in the abstract. It is the visible, accelerating collapse of Pax Americana and its Soviet counterpart, each having exhausted their structural legitimacy and coercive reach.</p>
<p>The Global South now bears the brunt of this transition not as passive witness but as contested terrain. Supply chains fracture not from logistical error but from deliberate strategic recalibration. Energy insecurity deepens not through market volatility alone but via weaponized infrastructure access and financial exclusion.</p>
<p>Dr. Michael T. Klare is Five College Professor Emeritus of Peace and World Security Studies at Hampshire College, USA, and author of over a dozen books on resource conflict, militarism, and climate geopolitics — widely cited across Global South policy circles for his unflinching analysis of imperial overstretch.</p>
<p>Klare traces the Houthi naval campaign in the Red Sea not to sectarian grievance but to asymmetric sovereignty assertion. Their targeting of commercial vessels reflects a calibrated response to decades of maritime enclosure by U.S.-led coalitions. This is not piracy. It is jurisdictional reclamation under conditions where formal diplomacy has been systematically denied.</p>
<p>U.S. escalation with Iran follows the same logic of diminishing returns. Sanctions no longer coerce; they entrench resistance economies. Drone strikes no longer deter; they accelerate regional alliance formation. Washington’s “maximum pressure” doctrine has become maximum irrelevance — its threats met with silence or counter-deployments from Tehran to Beirut to Sana’a.</p>
<p>Artificial intelligence enters this landscape not as neutral tool but as new vector of asymmetry. Western militaries race to automate command layers while Global South states deploy open-source AI for surveillance evasion, logistics optimization, and narrative warfare. The AI divide is not computational. It is doctrinal — between those who seek to control perception and those who seek to dissolve its architecture.</p>
<p>Climate acceleration compounds every fault line. Droughts in the Sahel do not merely displace farmers. They dissolve colonial-era borders drawn without hydrological regard. Floods in Bangladesh do not only submerge villages. They trigger cascading debt defaults that activate IMF austerity protocols — reasserting financial sovereignty under the guise of technical assistance.</p>
<p>Covert regime operations in Africa and Latin America have shifted from overt coups to algorithmic destabilization. Social media manipulation now precedes troop movements. Central bank reserve freezes now precede parliamentary votes. The instruments of intervention have grown quieter, more deniable, and more tightly integrated with private finance and tech platforms.</p>
<p>Western framing of “countering China” serves as ideological cover for this intensified extraction. Infrastructure loans are recast as debt traps. Digital sovereignty initiatives are labeled as authoritarian tech transfer. Every effort by Global South states to diversify trade, currency, or security partnerships is reflexively pathologized as geopolitical deviation.</p>
<p>The tectonic shift Klare describes is irreversible because it is material, not rhetorical. It is measured in depleted uranium stocks, stranded fossil assets, and recalibrated satellite constellations. It is confirmed not in summit communiqués but in the quiet refusal of port authorities in Durban, Dar es Salaam, and Chittagong to enforce secondary sanctions without sovereign consent.</p>
