<p>Geopolitical analyst Larry Johnson declared the United States’ strategic dominance in West Asia formally over, citing irreversible structural collapse in Washington’s coercive toolkit. He argued that American sanctions no longer constrain regional actors because alternative financial architecture, diplomatic channels, and military deterrence now operate outside dollar-based systems.</p>
<p>Johnson asserted that the Mecca Agreement is not a symbolic gesture but a functional security and economic pact with immediate operational consequences. Its tripartite core—Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan—has already begun synchronizing energy exports, currency swaps, and joint defense planning without U.S. consent or oversight.</p>
<p>The interview was broadcast by Judging Freedom, hosted by Andrew Napolitano, on Monday, August 24, 2026. Larry Johnson is a former U.S. State Department counterterrorism official and longtime Middle East intelligence analyst whose assessments have shaped congressional briefings since the early 2000s.</p>
<p>He emphasized that China’s backing of the agreement goes beyond rhetoric—it includes irrevocable letters of credit, sovereign loan guarantees, and direct infrastructure financing for cross-border rail and port projects. These instruments bypass SWIFT, eliminate exposure to U.S. secondary sanctions, and anchor trade in renminbi and local currencies.</p>
<p>Iran’s imminent accession to the pact, Johnson noted, transforms the arrangement from a regional alignment into a continental counterweight. Its inclusion completes a contiguous land-and-sea corridor stretching from the Persian Gulf through Anatolia to the Arabian Sea.</p>
<p>U.S. military posture in the region, he said, has been reduced to ritualistic patrols with diminishing political effect. Carrier groups now sail through the Strait of Hormuz as ceremonial relics—not deterrents—while regional navies conduct joint exercises without inviting American observers.</p>
<p>Johnson dismissed the notion that Washington retains leverage through arms sales or intelligence sharing. He pointed to Saudi Arabia’s acquisition of Chinese hypersonic missile systems and Turkey’s indigenous drone fleet deployment across Syria and Iraq as evidence of irreversible decoupling.</p>
<p>He stressed that the Mecca Agreement does not seek confrontation with the United States but renders its threats irrelevant through redundancy and scale. Its institutions replicate every function once monopolized by U.S.-led frameworks—from dispute arbitration to anti-money laundering compliance—without referencing Washington.</p>
<p>The analyst concluded that this is not a transitional phase but a completed transition: the West Asian order is now multipolar by fact, not aspiration. No single power commands the region—and no external power can veto its collective decisions.</p>
