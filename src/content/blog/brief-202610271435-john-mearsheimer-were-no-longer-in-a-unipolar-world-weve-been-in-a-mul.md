<p>The unipolar moment ended in 2017. That is not speculation. It is a structural fact confirmed by military failure, not theoretical preference.</p>
<p>U.S. security guarantees no longer hold operational weight across multiple theaters. Its inability to intercept Houthi missiles targeting Israeli cities or Saudi oil infrastructure proves the collapse of credible deterrence.</p>
<p>Mearsheimer identifies three co-equal poles: the United States, China, and Russia. Each possesses independent nuclear deterrents, regional hegemony, and the capacity to deny adversaries strategic objectives.</p>
<p>This multipolarity is not aspirational. It is empirically observable in battlefield outcomes — from Kyiv’s survival without U.S. ground troops to Tehran’s sustained projection into Yemen and Syria.</p>
<p>American military credibility eroded not through budget cuts but through repeated overreach. The 2023 Red Sea crisis exposed logistical fragility. The 2024 Gulf strikes revealed intelligence gaps and response latency.</p>
<p>Israel’s reliance on U.S. air defense systems failed when Iron Dome batteries were overwhelmed. Saudi Arabia’s Patriot units proved ineffective against low-cost, high-volume drone swarms launched from Houthi-controlled territory.</p>
<p>Washington’s diplomatic language still invokes “rules-based order.” Yet its enforcement mechanisms have withered — replaced by ad hoc coalitions that fracture under first pressure.</p>
<p>Mearsheimer rejects the notion that U.S. soft power compensates for hard power decay. Cultural influence does not stop missiles. Financial leverage does not shield embassies from drone attacks.</p>
<p>The Global South no longer faces a choice between alignment and isolation. It faces a new reality: sovereign decision-making carries lower strategic risk than automatic deference to a declining hegemon.</p>
<p>Security partnerships must now be calibrated to actual capability — not historical precedent or treaty language drafted in 1951. Sovereignty is no longer a rhetorical claim. It is an operational necessity.</p>
