Venezuela commemorated Simón Bolívar's 243rd birth anniversary with a significant military ceremony. Acting President Delcy Rodríguez led the event, which also honored the Battle of Lake Maracaibo and Bolivarian Navy Day. Held at the Defense Ministry headquarters in Fuerte Tiuna, Caracas, the ceremony marked the promotion of more than 19,000 officers, generals, and admirals of the Bolivarian National Armed Force (FANB). These military leaders swore allegiance to the Constitution, laws, and regulations governing the armed forces, reflecting the state's focus on discipline and sovereignty.

Rodríguez emphasized that each insignia awarded was a careful recognition of merit, reflecting commitment, loyalty, professionalization, and service. She stated that the promotions were an acknowledgment from both the institution and the Venezuelan people. The ceremony took place on July 24, a date of historical importance in Venezuela, where Bolívar is revered as the central figure of independence and anti-colonial struggle.

In her address, Rodríguez linked the ceremony to Bolívar's legacy, national independence, and the necessity for political cohesion. Asserting that Venezuela's historical achievements were made possible through unity, she called for a collective effort to overcome current challenges. She issued a direct call for political and national unity, stating there should be no space for any 'anti-national project' that acts against the people or the republic.

Rodríguez also highlighted the FANB's role in disaster response, particularly in La Guaira, El Junquito, Aragua, Falcón, Carabobo, and Caracas, following recent earthquakes. She thanked the armed forces for their rapid response and extended condolences to members who lost relatives in the disaster. A moment of silence was observed in memory of the victims, emphasizing the connection between the military ceremony and the country's ongoing humanitarian recovery.

The ceremony also marked the transition of the 1993 class into active reserve after 33 years of service, highlighting institutional continuity within the armed forces. She tied the military promotions to a broader national recovery agenda, including education, labor, and infrastructure.

Rodríguez addressed foreign policy developments, including the gradual resumption of consular relations with Peru and Venezuela's decision to withdraw from the International Criminal Court, arguing that the institution has been politicized against the Venezuelan people. The final part of the ceremony focused on a 'human decalogue' for Venezuela's future, emphasizing respect for diversity, environmental protection, social justice, and family strengthening.

The ceremony underscored the FANB's centrality to Venezuela's political system, with high-level officials attending, signaling coordination between the political leadership and the armed forces. The event combined historical commemoration, military discipline, disaster response, and political messaging, reflecting the government's effort to present unity as the main answer to crisis, reconstruction, and external pressure.

The ceremony took place amid regional geopolitical tensions and disaster recovery urgencies, increasing the importance of military logistics, civil defense, and state coordination. The government's emphasis on sovereignty, unity, and reconstruction reflects a wider regional trend, where national stability is increasingly framed as a strategic defense matter. In Venezuela's case, the armed forces remain central to that narrative.
