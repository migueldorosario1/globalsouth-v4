Iran has accused the European Union of hypocrisy following the bloc's decision to impose sanctions on six Iranian citizens. According to telesurenglish.net, the EU's move came in response to alleged 'human rights violations.' However, Iran argues that Brussels has not condemned the war crimes committed by the United States and Israel.

Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman, Ismail Bagaei, took to social media to challenge the EU's stance. He questioned the EU's ability to reconcile its commitment to human rights with its logistical and technical support for aggressions targeting Iranian civilians and infrastructure. Bagaei's comments were directed at EU foreign policy chief, Kaja Kallas.

Bagaei criticized the EU for not denouncing what he called 'the most blatant war crimes,' and for showing no solidarity with Iranian children killed by U.S. and Israeli attacks. He concluded that the EU's behavior was not just a loss of credibility but represented 'the banality of evil and the purest form of hypocrisy.'

The EU's new sanctions package, announced on Friday, targeted six Iranian nationals, which included five judges and an individual accused of being involved in multiple cyberattacks.

This development highlights the ongoing tensions between Iran and Western powers, with Iran demanding a more consistent application of human rights standards and decrying what it perceives as a double standard in the condemnation of human rights abuses. As the geopolitical landscape continues to evolve, the implications of such sanctions and the EU's approach to Iran will undoubtedly be a subject of intense scrutiny and debate.
