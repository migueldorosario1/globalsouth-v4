Spain faces a severe environmental and humanitarian crisis as two large forest fires ravage the bordering provinces of Madrid and Ávila, central Spain, forcing the evacuation of 73,000 people and burning approximately 25,000 hectares of forest, according to telesurenglish.net. These fires, exacerbated by favorable atmospheric conditions, are being managed as a single disaster by the Spanish government to streamline the national emergency response.

The President of the Spanish Government, Pedro Sánchez, announced the consolidation of the fires under a unified command structure to better coordinate the emergency efforts. The chief general of the Military Emergency Unit (UME), Francisco Javier Marcos, supported this decision as a means to 'gain time' in a situation that has already seen thousands of hectares burned.

The municipality of Cenicientos, initially chosen as the site for the unified command, was itself evacuated due to the fires, necessitating a relocation to Navalcarnero. The fires have led to the evacuation of 38,000 people from Ávila and 35,000 from Madrid, with thousands more confined to their homes. Among the newly evacuated are 30,000 residents from nine towns in the Tiétar river valley, an area of significant environmental importance.

Sánchez, who visited the affected areas and attended a meeting of the State Committee for Coordination and Management of the State Emergency Plan, noted that the situation 'remains complex' due to the unpredictability of wind direction and intensity, which hampers efforts to control the fires. He also pointed out that in 2026, Spain has already seen 130,000 hectares burned by fires, including 5,000 hectares on the day of his statement. This严峻的数据凸显了对抗气候紧急情况的紧迫性。

Provisional data from the Ministry of the Interior indicates that between 13,000 and 15,000 hectares have been burned in the province of Ávila, and nearly 10,000 in the province of Madrid. However, regional authorities in Madrid are already citing figures as high as 12,000 hectares.

The fires have spread rapidly, driven by strong and changing gusts of wind reaching speeds of up to 60 kilometers per hour. Spain has requested European aid to combat the fires, with assistance coming in the form of planes from Greece, Italy, and a detachment from Portugal.
