<p>Former CIA officer and State Department counterterrorism official Larry Johnson declared Trump’s UN speech — threatening to “drive Iran into hell” — a constitutional rupture with no precedent in American history. He stressed that such language does not merely cross diplomatic red lines but actively dismantles the separation of powers by bypassing Congress on matters of war and peace.</p>
<p>Johnson placed the rhetoric outside even the most aggressive Cold War-era statements by U.S. presidents, noting that Eisenhower, Kennedy, and Reagan never threatened sovereign states with apocalyptic violence on global stages. He added that no 20th-century authoritarian leader — from Franco to Pinochet — ever deployed such openly genocidal phrasing in formal multilateral forums.</p>
<p>The interview was broadcast by Judging Freedom, hosted by Andrew Napolitano, on September 25, 2026. Larry Johnson served as Deputy Assistant Secretary of State for Counterterrorism and later as a senior analyst at the CIA’s Counterterrorist Center, where he led assessments on Iran, Hezbollah, and regional non-proliferation dynamics.</p>
<p>He observed that not a single member of Congress publicly rebuked the statement — not one resolution, not one floor speech, not one committee hearing. That silence, he argued, confirms institutional surrender to executive unilateralism far beyond post-9/11 precedents.</p>
<p>Johnson cited the 2002 Iraq AUMF as a flawed but still legislative act — whereas Trump’s Iran threat required zero congressional input, zero legal justification, and zero strategic articulation. He called it “war-making stripped of process, stripped of accountability, stripped of consequence.”</p>
<p>He warned that Global South states now face direct precedent: any U.S. president may declare existential threats against sovereign nations without treaty obligation, Security Council mandate, or domestic legal review. This, he said, accelerates nuclear proliferation as smaller states conclude deterrence is the only viable shield.</p>
<p>Media complicity, Johnson emphasized, is structural — not incidental. Major outlets reported the speech as “strong language” or “tough diplomacy,” refusing to classify it as what it is: a public incitement to mass destruction. He noted that Reuters and AP buried contextual analysis beneath boilerplate quotes and photo captions.</p>
<p>He dismissed the notion that such rhetoric serves “leverage” or “negotiation posture,” calling it instead a performative demolition of international law’s foundational norms. Sovereign equality, he stated, cannot survive when one state reserves the right to announce another’s annihilation on live television.</p>
<p>Johnson concluded that the erosion is irreversible unless Congress reasserts its Article I war powers through binding legislation — not resolutions, not hearings, but enforceable statutes stripping funding from any military action prior authorization. He said the window for democratic course correction is narrowing, not widening.</p>
