<p>In a recent broadcast on the channel Daniel Davis / Deep Dive, host Daniel Davis offered a sharp critique of Donald Trump's latest foreign policy claims. During a media appearance, Donald Trump asserted that the war with Iran is scheduled to end immediately after the United States presidential election. He argued that Iran is desperate and unable to hold out any longer, claiming that the country's primary goal is to interfere in the election to secure a weaker administration that would allow them to acquire a nuclear weapon. Davis, however, strongly dismissed these claims as delusional and disconnected from historical and geopolitical realities.</p><p>According to the channel Daniel Davis / Deep Dive, the former president's assessment ignores Iran's long-standing resilience under extreme pressure. In the analysis conducted by Daniel Davis, the host pointed out that Iran survived an intense eight-year war with Iraq and withstood the maximum pressure campaign during Donald Trump's first term. Despite severe economic sanctions, military blockades, and targeted strikes on its infrastructure, Iran has not been brought to its knees. Davis highlighted that Iran continues to maintain robust ballistic missile and long-range drone manufacturing capabilities, consistently responding to American and allied strikes with a tit-for-tat strategy.</p><p>During the analysis, Daniel Davis explained that the fundamental economic factors do not support the idea of an imminent Iranian collapse. Iran possesses abundant natural resources, produces enough food to sustain its population of 95 million, and holds vast petroleum reserves. While domestic gasoline prices in Tehran have seen minor adjustments, the country is far from the state of desperation that Donald Trump described. The host emphasized that expecting Iran to suddenly roll over or sue for peace immediately after the November elections is an irrational expectation lacking any logical foundation.</p><p>The host also warned of the dangerous escalatory path that current US policies are creating. Daniel Davis expressed concern that some political figures and commentators in the United States are trying to lower the threshold for using nuclear weapons, portraying them as just another tool in the military arsenal. Davis argued that pushing an enemy like Iran into a corner where they have nothing to lose will only trigger a massive retaliation. He referenced remarks by political analyst Mohammad Marandi, who noted that the Iranian leadership has shifted toward an offensive mentality and is prepared to strike back at regional economic targets if the blockade continues.</p><p>In addition to his predictions about Iran, Donald Trump promised that gasoline prices would fall below two dollars a gallon shortly after the elections. Daniel Davis characterized this as another fantasy, explaining that rising costs for diesel, jet fuel, and global shipping make such a drop highly improbable. Davis noted that the hawkish foreign policy of the US administration is actively harming its own economic interests, driving inflation higher and fueling public frustration over endless foreign conflicts.</p><p>The broadcast on the channel Daniel Davis / Deep Dive concluded with an analysis of the war in Ukraine, where Donald Trump claimed he could easily broker a peace deal between Russian President Vladimir Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky. Davis reminded viewers that Trump has made similar promises to end the conflict within 24 hours since early 2023, none of which have materialized. With Russia currently on the ascendant on the battlefield, Davis concluded that any diplomatic resolution will only occur on Moscow's terms, as stated by Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov, rather than through the unilateral deals proposed by Trump.</p>
