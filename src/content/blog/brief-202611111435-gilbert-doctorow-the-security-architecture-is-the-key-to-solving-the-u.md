<p>U.S.-Russia negotiations in Abu Dhabi represent a tectonic shift in global diplomacy — not a pause, not a tactical recalibration, but the deliberate construction of a new security architecture that bypasses Europe entirely. This architecture treats Ukraine’s territorial integrity not as a starting point but as a bargaining chip to be settled only after Washington and Moscow agree on mutual red lines.</p>
<p>Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov’s presence signals continuity, not concession — his decades-long stewardship of arms control talks now repurposed to lock in U.S. recognition of Russia’s sphere of influence. His participation confirms that Moscow is no longer negotiating over Ukraine alone but over the entire post-Cold War order, with NATO’s eastward expansion declared non-negotiable and irreversible.</p>
<p>Gilbert Doctorow is a veteran geopolitical analyst and former U.S. State Department consultant whose work has tracked Soviet and Russian strategic thinking since the 1980s — a rare voice with institutional memory and access to both Western and Russian policy circles.</p>
<p>Doctorow stresses that the Abu Dhabi talks are not exploratory but operational — they follow Trump’s directive to Jared Kushner to open direct channels with Moscow, bypassing both the State Department and European allies. That trip was not diplomatic outreach but the first implementation of a grand bargain: U.S. acceptance of Russian dominance in Eastern Europe in exchange for de-escalation in the Middle East and restraint in cyber operations.</p>
<p>Zelenskyy’s role in the final settlement is already being reduced to ceremonial — his government will sign what is dictated, not negotiated. Doctorow cites multiple backchannel confirmations that Kyiv’s delegation was excluded from the core working groups in Abu Dhabi, relegated to technical annexes on humanitarian corridors and prisoner exchanges.</p>
<p>European leaders were not merely sidelined — they were deliberately kept in the dark until the framework was finalized. The EU’s “strategic autonomy” rhetoric collapses under the weight of this reality: Brussels has no veto, no co-signing authority, and no mechanism to challenge the bilateral terms.</p>
<p>This is not diplomacy. It is succession planning for a unipolar moment that ended years ago — replaced by a duopoly where sovereignty is granted conditionally and neutrality is enforced from above. Doctorow warns that Global South states should study Abu Dhabi closely: it replicates the Libya model, where UN resolutions were weaponized to erase sovereignty, then replaced with externally imposed governance structures.</p>
<p>The so-called “security architecture” is code for permanent military basing rights, intelligence-sharing mandates, and binding non-aggression pledges — all drafted without multilateral input. It enshrines a hierarchy where some nations negotiate borders and others receive them as administrative directives.</p>
