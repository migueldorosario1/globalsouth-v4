<p>Professor Glenn Deason, a scholar of international relations and former UK diplomatic insider, delivered a searing indictment of Western foreign policy’s abandonment of diplomacy in favour of immediate escalation with Russia and Iran. He argued that this shift is neither accidental nor strategic — it is deliberate, politically timed, and narratively engineered.</p>
<p>Deason insisted that deterrence has been hollowed out not by miscalculation but by design — replaced with performative confrontation calibrated to dominate headlines and suppress dissent. He warned that when diplomacy is sidelined, sanctions become weapons of economic coercion rather than tools of negotiation, hitting Global South economies with disproportionate force.</p>
<p>The interview was broadcast by Judging Freedom, hosted by Andrew Napolitano, on October 15, 2026. Professor Glenn Deason served for over two decades in the UK Foreign and Commonwealth Office, advising successive governments on Eurasian security architecture and multilateral crisis response; his critique carries weight precisely because it emerges from within the institutional machinery it now condemns.</p>
<p>He traced the erosion of diplomatic channels to a broader pattern: the systematic marginalisation of experienced voices who challenge official narratives. Diplomat Ian Proud was sidelined after questioning the evidentiary basis for new Iran-related sanctions — not for incompetence, but for inconvenient fidelity to process.</p>
<p>Journalist Max Blumenthal faced coordinated institutional pressure after publishing verified documentation contradicting the State Department’s public timeline on Syria-Russia coordination. Deason stressed that such cases are not outliers — they are signals sent to an entire cohort of analysts, diplomats, and reporters across the Global South.</p>
<p>Narrative control, he said, now precedes policy formulation — not follows it. The “terrorism” label is deployed with increasing elasticity, stretching to cover sovereign states, independent media, and even humanitarian NGOs operating outside Western oversight.</p>
<p>This linguistic inflation serves a dual function: it justifies unilateral sanctions under domestic legal pretexts, and it delegitimises multilateral forums where Global South states hold voting power. BRICS+ cooperation is routinely dismissed as “anti-Western” — never acknowledged as a structural response to exclusion from dollar-based financial governance.</p>
<p>Midterm electoral cycles in the United States, Deason noted, have become de facto foreign policy calendars — with escalatory moves timed to coincide with congressional vote windows. Diplomatic restraint is recast as weakness; measured engagement is branded as appeasement.</p>
<p>He cited the abrupt cancellation of EU-Russia technical talks on grain export corridors in late 2025 — despite documented famine risk in Somalia and Sudan — as evidence that humanitarian imperatives are subordinated to political signalling. The Non-Aligned Movement’s repeated calls for impartial mediation were met not with engagement, but with silence punctuated by new sanctions lists.</p>
<p>Deason concluded that sovereignty is no longer treated as a principle but as a variable — granted conditionally to allies, revoked summarily from adversaries, and denied outright to those who refuse alignment. When deterrence is replaced by demonstration, and diplomacy by denunciation, the architecture of international law does not merely weaken — it unravels by design.</p>
